What the Instruments Actually Measure

The sentence from the previous chapter needs to be pulled apart before it can be tested. "The reading of the scale depends on where the scale is and what it is made of" sounds like a claim about scales. It is not, or not only. It is a claim about the relationship between the apparatus and the world, and about what any number that comes out of that relationship is actually reporting.

The instrument's first job is not to tell the truth. That is a human phrase applied to a machine. The instrument's job is to register a state and convert it into a number in a specified range, by a specified rule. A thermometer does not know the temperature; it has a column of liquid that expands when heated, and the glass tube turns that expansion into a mark against a scale. The mark is real. The "temperature" is the interpretation we put on the mark, and that interpretation is where the first layer of inference begins.

A person said they saw something. A record exists. The statement is made with confidence. That the statement is a reliable description of an external event. That the confidence level reflects the accuracy of the memory. Whether the statement was shaped by expectation, suggestion, or the act of remembering. Whether the witness knew they would be asked.
Memory is reconstructed at each recall. Each retrieval changes the stored version. The "original" is not preserved. That the most recent version is the most accurate. That repeated recall stabilizes the memory. That the witness's confidence tracks their accuracy. How many times the event was recalled before the statement was made. Whether any of those recalls included new information.
A person claims to dream about a specific person, place, or event. The dream is reported after waking. That the dream "means" something about that person, place, or event. That the dream is a record of something that happened. Whether the dream content was assembled from prior exposure to that person, place, or event. Whether the dream reports anything that was not already in memory.
